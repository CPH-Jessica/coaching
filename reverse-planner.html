Packet completeness

0 · Ground rules

You are not starting over. You are mining what you already wrote. Sunk cost is not a plot device. If a chapter does not earn its pages, it becomes a note.

What this desk does: extracts the book hiding in the six chapters, locks the five core elements + story promise, and builds a handoff packet for the CPH Fiction Planner.
What it does not do: outline forward until we know whether chapter 6 is even the right chapter 6.

2 · Mine the six chapters

What is actually on the pages — not what you meant to write three years ago.

Protagonist on page 1

Name + one line. Who they are when we meet them, not who they become.

What do they WANT on page 1?

External. Visible. “Peace” is not a want.

What do they actually NEED?

If you do not know, write that. Need is usually the opposite of the armor.

Love interest / second lead

Name, when they enter, the friction — not the aesthetic.

Opposing force

Person, system, clock, or self. If the only opposition is “feelings,” you have a mood, not a plot.

Inciting incident

The event that makes the old life impossible. Point at the chapter or admit it has not happened.

By the end of chapter 6, what cannot be undone?

If the answer is “she’s in a different room,” that is not change.

The scene that still has a pulse
The scene you suspect is stalling
The question you have been dodging

Right size: “Why would she stay after he lies in chapter 4?” Wrong size: “How do they end up together?”

Chapter inventory

One sentence each. Keep = earns its pages. Salvage = a moment lives, the chapter dies. Cut = useful only as a note.

3 · The honest stop

The planner cannot fix a motivation problem and a premise problem with the same tool.

Why did you stop? Check every one that is true.
What did you hope would happen that didn’t?
If you could keep only three things from these six chapters, what are they?

A voice. A kiss. A setting. A line. A secret. Be ruthless.

4 · Five core elements

This is Phase 1. If the draft and the element disagree, the element wins and the draft gets revised.

1. Genre lock

The version you would put on a product page. One line.

2. Tropes — three to five. Not six.

Muddy promise = muddy blurb = muddy reader trust.

3. Hook / concept

Why THIS book, not the other twelve on the same shelf.

4. Stakes

What breaks if they fail. “They won’t get together” is not stakes. What does the getting-together cost?

5. Emotional core

What should the reader feel in the last ten pages, and why does that feeling matter?

5 · Story promise

Read it out loud. The blank that makes you wince is the one you have not decided.

This is a ______ about
______ who are forced to
forced to ______
while hiding ______
leading to ______
This is a — about — who are forced to — while hiding —, leading to —.

7 · Structural honesty

Six chapters is usually a baggy Act 1. Sometimes it is three openings and a meet-cute. Rarely you already blew past midpoint — that is why it feels stuck.
